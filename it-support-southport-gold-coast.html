Local landscape

What Southport is actually like to work in

Southport is the closest thing the Gold Coast has to a traditional CBD, and it behaves like one. The Southport Courthouse — Magistrates, District and Supreme — anchors a genuine legal precinct, with barristers' chambers and law firms clustered through Nerang Street, Scarborough Street and the surrounding blocks. Where there are courts there are also the accountants, forensic specialists, mediators and consultants that orbit them.

A short distance north, the health precinct around the Gold Coast University Hospital and the Griffith University campus produces a second concentration — specialist suites, allied health, diagnostic services and the medical administration businesses that support them. That is a very different IT problem to a law firm, and both are within ten minutes of each other.

Then there is the commercial core: Australia Fair and the retail through Scarborough Street, Chinatown along Young and Davenport Streets with its hospitality density, and the government and not-for-profit offices scattered through the older towers. The Broadwater Parklands and the Aquatic Centre bring events and the operators that service them.

The building stock is the most varied on the coast. Southport has purpose-built modern towers, 1980s office blocks that have been through three fit-outs, and converted premises that started life as something else entirely. What is actually in the walls varies enormously from one address to the next, and it is worth establishing before planning anything.

What's technically different about Southport

The building stock is the real variable. Southport ranges from modern towers with proper comms rooms to 1980s blocks that have been refitted repeatedly, with cabling from four different decades layered on top of each other. We have opened ceilings in Southport and found Cat3, Cat5, Cat5e and Cat6 in the same tray, none of it labelled. Assume nothing about what is in the walls until it is tested — that assumption is where budgets go wrong.

High-rise access needs planning. Several of the commercial towers require building management approval, a booked service lift and often an after-hours window for anything involving cabling or equipment being moved. We arrange that in advance rather than discovering it on the day, the same way we do in Surfers Paradise.

Confidentiality changes the design. In a legal or medical practice, file access structured by role rather than open to everyone stops being a nice-to-have. So does managed device control on anything that leaves the office, and secure remote access rather than something published to the internet. These are the specific requirements that make a Southport fit-out different to a general office.

Parking genuinely affects response time. Unglamorous, but it is the difference between a technician reaching your desk in five minutes or twenty. We know where to park around the courts precinct and Australia Fair, which sounds trivial until you have waited for someone who did not.

What IT support in Southport actually involves

A representative engagement, drawn from real work with identifying detail removed — we don’t name clients without written permission.

Representative engagement

A Southport practice that could not say who opened a file

The situation

A professional practice in the Southport legal and medical precinct was asked by a client to describe how it controls access to confidential files. It could not answer with any confidence.

What we found

Four staff shared a single login on the reception machine, which also held saved credentials to the practice’s document system. The audit trail existed and recorded every action against the same shared account, so it was complete and useless. The practice had assumed shared access was a reasonable arrangement for a small team.

What we did

Created named accounts for every person with multi-factor authentication, removed saved credentials from the shared machine, and set out a short written position on access that the practice could give to a client who asks.

The outcome

The practice can now answer the question it was asked. Southport’s concentration of legal, medical and accounting practices means client-driven security questions arrive here earlier than in most parts of the coast.
