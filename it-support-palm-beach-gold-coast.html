Local landscape

What Palm Beach is actually like to work in

Palm Beach has changed faster than almost anywhere on the southern Gold Coast. The strip along the Gold Coast Highway and through Nineteenth Avenue has filled with independent cafes, restaurants, retail and studios that were not there a decade ago — occupying commercial premises that mostly were.

That gap is the recurring theme. The buildings are older, frequently repurposed more than once, and their services reflect whatever the previous tenant needed rather than what a modern business does. Cabling is improvised or absent. There is rarely anywhere sensible to put equipment. Wireless has to cover through original construction that predates anyone thinking about signal.

The business mix is overwhelmingly small — two to ten people is typical, with a lot of owner-operators. Hospitality and food along the highway and toward the Palm Beach Parklands and Tallebudgera Creek. Retail through the strip. Creative studios, agencies and consultants drawn by the location and the rents. And a growing layer of small professional and wellness practices.

Very few of these businesses have any internal IT, and many have no provider at all until something breaks in a way they cannot work around.

What's technically different about Palm Beach

Premises predate the businesses in them. Most commercial space along the strip has been repurposed at least once. Cabling is frequently improvised, run where it could be rather than where it should be, and equipment ends up wherever there was a spare power point. What is actually installed varies enormously between neighbouring shopfronts, so we survey rather than assume.

Remote support matters more here than anywhere. At twenty-five minutes out, a $100 + GST call-out is a real proportion of a small job — sometimes most of it. That is why we genuinely try remote first rather than saying we do: remote is $190 + GST per hour with no call-out, and most email, Microsoft 365, software, account and printer faults never need anyone to travel. We will tell you on the phone which yours is likely to be.

Most Palm Beach businesses do not need managed IT. We say that to more clients here than in any other suburb. With a handful of laptops, everything in the cloud, no server and no compliance obligations, a monthly fee buys very little. Ad-hoc support at an hourly rate is usually the honest recommendation — and we would rather lose the recurring revenue than sell something that is not warranted.

Two things are worth doing regardless of size. Multi-factor authentication on every mailbox, and a backup you have actually watched restore. Between them they prevent most of what genuinely damages a small business, and neither costs much.

What IT support in Palm Beach actually involves

A representative engagement, drawn from real work with identifying detail removed — we don’t name clients without written permission.

Representative engagement

A Palm Beach practice on a connection with nothing behind it

The situation

A small practice on the Palm Beach strip had moved its booking system, files and phones into the cloud over several years. A single internet service carried all of it, which nobody had thought about in those terms.

What we found

Reviewing the setup before anything went wrong, an outage of any length would stop every function of the business at once — including the phone customers would use to ask what was happening. The premises were on a fibre-to-the-node service with copper for the final stretch, which in this part of the coast is the section most affected by weather.

What we did

Installed a router with automatic mobile failover, tested it by disconnecting the primary service during a quiet period, and confirmed calls stayed connected through the switchover.

The outcome

A street fault some months later cost the practice a slower afternoon rather than a closed day. The equipment cost considerably less than the day would have.
